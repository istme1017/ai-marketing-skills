# Clip selection — how Klap finds the moments

Klap's real edge is not the captions, it's the selection. It reads the whole transcript and pulls only moments that survive on their own with zero context. Do the same: work from the transcript, never by scrubbing the video.

## What counts as a clippable moment

A moment qualifies when it is ALL of these:

- **Self-contained.** A viewer who has never seen the long video understands it completely. No "as I said earlier", no unresolved pronouns ("that's why he failed" — who?). If the moment needs setup, either include the setup inside the clip or drop the moment.
- **One idea.** One story, one claim, one tip, one hot take. Two ideas = two clips.
- **Opens hot.** The first sentence works as a hook on its own (see hook rules below). If the payoff sentence is in the middle, cut so the clip STARTS at or just before it.
- **15–60 seconds.** Sweet spot 25–45s. Under 15s feels like a fragment; over 60s loses Shorts/Reels eligibility and retention.
- **Ends clean.** Last sentence lands the point. Never fade out mid-thought; trim to the exact word.

## Moment types that reliably clip (in order of strength)

1. Contrarian claim / hot take ("Everyone tells you X. That's wrong.")
2. Specific numbers or money ("We went from $3k to $80k a month doing one thing")
3. Story with a turn (setup → problem → punchline/lesson, compressed)
4. Tactical how-to with a countable payoff ("3 steps", "the exact script")
5. Emotional spike — laughter, conflict, confession, disbelief
6. Question the audience is already asking, answered directly

## The virality score (0–100)

Klap stamps every clip with a score. Reproduce it with this rubric, and report it per clip:

| Factor | Weight | What earns points |
|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | 30 | First 3 seconds create a curiosity gap, a bold claim, or a named payoff |
| Self-containment | 20 | Zero external context needed; no dangling references |
| Emotion/energy | 20 | Surprise, humor, conflict, conviction in delivery |
| Specificity | 15 | Numbers, names, exact steps, concrete outcomes |
| Pacing | 15 | No dead air > 1.5s, no rambling detours, ends on the point |

- 85–100: post as-is, lead candidate.
- 70–84: strong, post it.
- 50–69: post only if the source video has few better moments; tighten the cut first.
- Below 50: don't cut it. Klap shows these clips too, but you shouldn't waste renders on them.

## Hook rules (first 3 seconds)

- The clip must open mid-energy, on the hook sentence — not on a breath, a filler word, or the tail of the previous sentence. Trim "so, um, yeah" to zero.
- If the speaker buries the hook ("...and that's when I realized nobody actually reads the pricing page"), start the clip AT that sentence and let the explanation follow. Cold-open on the payoff is a standard Klap move.
- Write a one-line title/hook for each clip (this becomes the caption/description when posting). Formats that work: "Nobody tells you this about X", "How he did X in Y days", "The X mistake everyone makes", a verbatim quote of the boldest sentence.

## How many clips

- Default: every moment scoring 70+, capped at 10 per source video (Klap's default batch).
- If the user asks for N clips, deliver the top N by score.
- Never pad the batch with sub-50 moments to hit a count. Say "this video has 4 real clips" instead.

## Cutting discipline

- Cut on word boundaries from the transcript timestamps, then pad **start −0.15s** and **end +0.25s** so words aren't clipped.
- Kill internal dead air: if a silence inside the clip exceeds ~1.5s, cut it out (jump cut). Klap clips feel fast because the silences are gone.
- Never let the clip end on a filler ("so yeah", "you know what I mean"). End on the landing sentence.
