# Amazon Interview Prep — Ariel Ferrer

Amazon interviews are **almost entirely Leadership Principles (LP) behavioral questions**, scored in STAR format. This is not like most interviews — the interviewer is filling in a scoring rubric while you talk, and vague answers score zero regardless of how good your actual experience is. Prepare these and you will outperform candidates with stronger resumes.

---

## ⚠️ Read this before you use any of it

**The stories below are scaffolded from your resume, but the specific numbers are blanks you must fill in yourself.** I know you led 80–100 associates and saved about 2 hours a day with automation, because your resume says so. I do **not** know your rate percentages, your defect numbers, or what happened on any particular shift.

Every `[BLANK]` is a place where you need a real number or detail. **Do not invent them.** Amazon interviewers ask relentless follow-up questions — "how did you measure that?", "what was the number before?", "who else was involved?" — and a fabricated detail collapses in about two questions. A modest true story beats an impressive false one every time, and Amazon specifically trains interviewers to probe for this.

If you can't remember a number, say the honest version: *"I don't remember the exact figure, but the pattern was..."* That scores fine. Making one up does not.

---

## The STAR format Amazon scores against

| Letter | What they want | How long |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | Context — where, when, what was at stake | 15 sec |
| **T**ask | *Your* specific responsibility, not the team's | 10 sec |
| **A**ction | **What YOU did.** Say "I," not "we" | 60–90 sec |
| **R**esult | Quantified outcome + what you learned | 20 sec |

**The single most common failure is saying "we."** Amazon is scoring *you*. "We reduced errors" earns nothing; "I built the tracker, I trained the team on it, I audited the first two weeks" earns the point. Use "we" only for genuine credit-sharing, then immediately return to "I."

## Your five core stories

You need roughly five strong stories that you can re-angle for different LPs. These are built from what your resume actually documents.

### Story 1 — The automation (your strongest asset)

**Covers:** Invent and Simplify · Ownership · Dive Deep · Deliver Results

> **S:** At the Pasadena fulfillment center, my shift's reporting was manual. Every day I and other PAs spent roughly two hours pulling numbers into spreadsheets by hand to build the shift performance picture for leadership.
>
> **T:** Nobody asked me to fix it — it was accepted as part of the job. But the manual entry was costing us floor time during shift and introducing errors into the numbers leadership was making decisions on.
>
> **A:** I mapped where the data actually originated and found [BLANK — which systems/reports you pulled from]. I built [BLANK — describe what you built: an Excel macro? A script? A template?] that pulled and formatted it automatically. I tested it in parallel against the manual version for [BLANK — how long?] to confirm the numbers matched before trusting it. Then I [BLANK — did you train other PAs on it? Did it spread to other shifts?].
>
> **R:** It eliminated about two hours of manual work per day, which put me back on the floor with my associates during shift. Data-entry errors dropped [BLANK — can you characterize this at all? "noticeably"? A number?], and leadership got a more reliable picture of shift performance. [BLANK — was it adopted beyond your shift?]

**Why this story is strong:** it shows initiative on something nobody assigned you, which is the core of Ownership, plus technical capability that most Area Manager candidates don't have. Lead with this one when you get the chance.

**Expect follow-ups:** "How did you validate the numbers were right?" · "What broke when you first deployed it?" · "Why hadn't anyone done this before?" · "How did you get others to adopt it?"

### Story 2 — Coaching an underperforming associate

**Covers:** Hire and Develop the Best · Earn Trust · Insist on the Highest Standards

> **S:** I had an associate on my shift whose [BLANK — rate? quality? attendance?] was consistently below the expectation.
>
> **T:** As PA I owned their performance, and I wanted to fix the root cause rather than just document toward termination.
>
> **A:** I [BLANK — what did you actually do first? Watch them work? Talk to them privately?]. I found the underlying issue was [BLANK — was it training? Equipment? Something personal? A process problem?]. I [BLANK — specific intervention: retrained on a technique, moved them to a different path, adjusted their station, escalated to HR?]. I then [BLANK — how did you follow up? Daily check-ins? Weekly?].
>
> **R:** [BLANK — what happened? Did they improve? By how much? Did they stay?]

**If the honest answer is that it didn't work out** — use it anyway. Amazon respects a story where you did the right process and the person still didn't improve, as long as you show what you learned and that you were fair. That is often a *stronger* answer than a tidy success.

**Covers:** Earn Trust · Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

>
> **T:** [BLANK — what was your position, and why?]
>
> **A:** [BLANK — how did you raise it? What data did you bring? Who did you talk to?]

**Important:** "Disagree and Commit" means you can lose the argument and still execute the decision fully. A story where you pushed back with data, were overruled, and then implemented the decision properly anyway scores **very** well. Do not only prepare stories where you were right.

## Questions you will almost certainly be asked

**"Why do you want to come back to Amazon?"**
You left in January 2026 and are applying in August. They *will* ask, and it is the question that most needs a prepared answer. Be straight about why you left and what you want now. Do not badmouth the company or a manager — that reliably tanks an Amazon interview. If you left for a reason you'd rather not detail, a short honest frame works: *"I stepped away for [reason]. What I found is that I miss the pace and the scale, and I know how to operate in it."*

**"Tell me about a time you failed."**
Have a real one ready with a genuine lesson. Candidates who deflect this ("I work too hard") score badly. Amazon's Ownership principle explicitly includes owning mistakes.

**"Why should we hire you over an external candidate?"**
Your answer: you already know Amazon's systems, standards, metrics, and pace. You need no ramp on culture or tooling. You have led 80–100 associates inside an Amazon building specifically — you know what the job actually is on a bad night, not in theory.

**"Are you willing to work nights, weekends, and holidays?"**
This is a hard requirement in the Area Manager job description. Say yes clearly, and mean it.

## Your gap, and what to do about it

The Area Manager II posting lists as *preferred*: **"Experience with process improvements (Lean process, Kaizen, and/or Six Sigma)."** You do not have a formal credential.

Two moves:
1. **Reframe what you have.** Your automation project *is* process improvement — you eliminated waste in a manual process. You do not have the certificate, but you have done the work. Say it that way: *"I don't hold a formal belt, but the automation project was process-improvement work — I identified waste in a manual reporting process and eliminated it."*
2. **Close it cheaply.** A free or low-cost Six Sigma White or Yellow Belt takes a few hours online. If you have participated in any Amazon Kaizen event, name it — Amazon runs these constantly and internal participation counts.

## The night before

- Write your five stories out in full. **Say them aloud.** Reading is not the same as speaking them.
- Time yourself: 2–3 minutes per story. Rambling past four minutes loses the interviewer.
- Have your numbers in front of you. Amazon phone screens are not proctored — keep notes visible.
- Prepare two questions to ask them. Good ones: *"What does success look like in the first 90 days?"* and *"What's the biggest operational challenge this site is facing right now?"*
- Re-read the job description an hour before. Their language is what you should mirror back.
